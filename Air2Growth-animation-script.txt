AIR2GROWTH WEBSITE ANIMATION SCRIPT
Coding agent handoff

Create a 20-second illustrated journey for the existing Air2Growth website using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and SVG. This is the storyboard and implementation brief; build the finished animation from it. The attached Air2Growth-logo.svg is the vector asset to use.

The visitor clicks the exact button label “Animation Begin”. The website then guides them through the story by automatically scrolling a dedicated animation section. The scene stays pinned while its illustrations change. The visitor can pause, take over scrolling, or exit throughout.

The story must communicate six things: customers order the machine; an Air2Growth employee delivers and installs it; captured atmospheric CO₂ supports algae cultivation and liquid fertiliser production; customers use the integrated touchscreen; Air2Growth receives machine fault states through IoT; customers receive maintenance updates. Machine monitoring collects machine states only, with no other private information from the site.

CREATIVE DIRECTION

Use a warm, precise vector illustration style with layered scenery, expressive people, a branded service van, a greenhouse, crop rows, tools, pipes, liquid, and an easy-to-understand machine cutaway. The overall feeling should be human and useful. Keep the engineering process recognisable without presenting a fabrication drawing.

Default language: English, using British spelling “fertiliser”. Default audio: silent; every idea must work visually. Default duration: 20 seconds of uninterrupted guided playback. Manual scrolling and pauses can make a visit longer.

Use the supplied stacked Air2Growth wordmark and “FROM CARBON TO CROP” tagline. Preserve its geometry, proportions, and lettering. The SVG is a trace of the supplied JPG, with outlined lettering, a transparent background, and no embedded raster image or font dependency. Keep the small tagline out of tiny applications; use the full logo at a readable size on the opening and closing frames. Reveal the logo as a whole rather than rearranging its letters.

Brand colours sampled from the supplied artwork:
- Charcoal: #2F2F2F
- Dark green: #409530
- Medium green: #68B930
- Lime: #B0E229

Supporting illustration colours: warm off-white backgrounds, pale blue water, and amber for service alerts. Use charcoal for text. Lime is an accent, not small text on white. Keep gas movement, liquid flow, and telemetry visually distinct: charcoal CO₂ particles, blue/green liquid, and dashed green data lines.

CAST AND SETTING

Carry the same customer and the same Air2Growth delivery/installation employee through the entire journey. The employee wears a green uniform with a clearly recognisable brand mark; the customer wears farm work clothes. Give each a distinct silhouette.

Add a depot packer, a farm assistant, two background farm workers, and a remote support engineer: seven people across the story. Give each a meaningful activity, such as securing a crate, moving supplies, tending plants, or checking a status screen. Show no more than two main foreground actions at once. Background people should support the story without competing with the caption.

The site is a farm with a greenhouse and adjacent crops. Use one consistent machine enclosure, with a visible attached touchscreen, air intake, cultivation area, and product collection point. A schematic cutaway can reveal a capture cartridge and three cultivation vessels. These are explanatory shapes, not claims about final enclosure dimensions or pipe routing. Do not invent exact hardware specifications.

PREVIEW STATE BEFORE PLAYBACK

Show the logo, the customer beside the installed machine, and the heading “From carbon to crop”. Display:

Button: “Animation Begin”
Helper text: “20-second guided tour · Pause or exit any time”

The website scrolls normally before the button is activated. Do not reserve a long empty scroll runway or start the sequence just because the visitor reaches this section. Keep the illustration poster still until activation.

MASTER TIMELINE

Times below are absolute positions in one 20-second timeline. Transitions are included in these intervals; do not add extra transition time between scenes.

Scene 1: 00.0–02.0 seconds — Order
Scene 2: 02.0–04.0 seconds — Delivery by Air2Growth
Scene 3: 04.0–06.5 seconds — Installation by Air2Growth
Scene 4: 06.5–11.5 seconds — From ambient air to liquid fertiliser
Scene 5: 11.5–14.0 seconds — Integrated touchscreen monitoring
Scene 6: 14.0–16.5 seconds — IoT fault detection
Scene 7: 16.5–18.5 seconds — Maintenance and customer updates
Scene 8: 18.5–20.0 seconds — Closing farm view

SCENE 1 — ORDER — 00.0–02.0

Caption: “Order your Air2Growth”

00.0–00.5: Establish the farmer at a desk or workbench, with a greenhouse visible beyond the window. A laptop shows a simple Air2Growth product card. The machine silhouette on that card matches the machine used later.

00.5–01.3: The customer presses an illustrated “Order” button. A tick confirms the order. This is part of the animation, not a working checkout or form.

01.3–02.0: A small confirmation tile becomes a shipping label attached to the machine crate. The customer looks pleased; avoid a long facial animation or extra copy.

Transition: Follow the labelled crate into an Air2Growth service van. A single camera pan establishes the depot and the packer securing it. Reuse the crate rather than crossfading to an unrelated picture.

SCENE 2 — DELIVERY — 02.0–04.0

Caption: “Delivered by our team”

02.0–02.6: The Air2Growth employee closes the van door. The uniform and van both carry the brand mark. The packer gives a small wave.

02.6–03.4: The van travels along a short illustrated road as fields and the greenhouse move behind it. Rotate the wheels and use restrained parallax. The journey is symbolic, not a real map or tracked location.

03.4–04.0: The same employee arrives at the farm and wheels the protected machine towards the customer. A farm assistant opens the access gate.

Transition: Let the van pass briefly across the foreground to wipe into the installation position. Keep the employee, customer, greenhouse, and machine consistent.

SCENE 3 — INSTALLATION — 04.0–06.5

Caption: “Installed by Air2Growth”

04.0–04.8: The employee unwraps and positions the machine on a prepared base near the greenhouse. Show the customer observing rather than doing the installation.

04.8–05.7: The employee connects simplified power and water symbols, checks the enclosure, and activates the attached touchscreen. Show a small commissioning checklist with three ticks, without specifications or compliance claims.

05.7–06.5: The employee demonstrates the touchscreen to the customer. The device status changes to “Ready”. A short handover gesture makes the service feel personal.

Transition: Move closer to the installed machine and fade its front panel to a cutaway. Keep its exterior outline visible so the visitor knows they are seeing inside the same device.

SCENE 4 — HOW IT WORKS — 06.5–11.5

Caption, held through the scene: “From air to liquid fertiliser”
Small persistent label: “Time-lapse”

06.5–07.5: Ambient air enters the capture section. A few particles labelled CO₂ are retained in a cartridge while the remainder of the air continues past it. This should suggest selective capture, not that all incoming air turns into fertiliser.

07.5–08.3: A brief warm-coloured pulse illustrates a separate release/regeneration step. Concentrated CO₂ moves through a pipe towards the cultivation vessels. Distinguish capture and release as stages of a cycle; do not depict the same cartridge capturing and releasing simultaneously.

08.3–09.7: Show CO₂, water, nutrients, and light supporting algae growth. Use one sun/light icon, a water droplet, and a nutrient droplet, then let the culture become greener. Keep the small input labels readable and group them close to the vessels. The “Time-lapse” label establishes that growth takes real operating time.

09.7–11.5: Follow the green culture through a simple harvesting/preparation chamber and into a liquid fertiliser container. Label the container “Algae-based fertiliser”. A small visual continuation shows a farm worker applying the prepared product near plants, without dosage or yield claims.

Represent the process in this order:
ambient air → CO₂ capture and release → algae cultivation → harvesting and preparation → liquid algae-based fertiliser.

Water, nutrients, and light enter the cultivation step. Keep them visible enough that the animation does not imply that CO₂ alone provides all fertiliser nutrients. Do not show a direct pipe converting raw air into a bottle of fertiliser. Keep other plant/environmental benefit claims out of the sequence unless supplied separately.

Transition: A status light on the machine becomes the status indicator on the touchscreen. The cutaway closes and the camera moves to the actual attached display.

SCENE 5 — CUSTOMER TOUCHSCREEN — 11.5–14.0

Caption: “Check the integrated touchscreen”

11.5–12.3: Show the customer beside the machine. Enlarge the attached touchscreen through a camera push-in while leaving part of its mounting and the cabinet visible. It must clearly be integrated into the machine.

12.3–13.4: The customer touches the screen. Four simple status cards appear together: “Status”, “Production”, “Tank level”, and “Energy”. Use icons, status words, and simple bars. Avoid invented live measurements or annual production numbers. Include a subtle “Illustrative display” label.

13.4–14.0: The customer checks a green “Running” state. Small operating-state indicators form a dashed data line leading out of the machine.

Transition: Follow this dashed line to an IoT cloud and the Air2Growth support dashboard. Keep the physical touchscreen visible as a small inset so local and remote monitoring are easy to distinguish.

SCENE 6 — IOT FAULT DETECTION — 14.0–16.5

Caption: “Faults reach our support team”

14.0–14.8: A small component icon changes to amber and the local display reports “Pump fault”. The same state travels as a small packet through the IoT cloud. Use a warning triangle and a text label; colour alone must not convey the fault.

14.8–15.7: The remote support engineer sees the affected machine highlighted on an Air2Growth dashboard. The dashboard shows machine state and fault information. It does not show a camera feed, people tracking, farm imagery, an address, or personal records. The illustrated scene is a sample workflow, not a promise of continuous human observation.

15.7–16.5: The customer's integrated screen shows “Service needed”. Connect the notification to the same fault. This establishes that the customer is informed as well as the support team.

At 14.0 seconds, introduce a compact privacy panel and keep it legible through the end of the tour, including the closing hold:

Panel label: “Machine monitoring”
Panel message: “Machine states only. No private information.”

Use a simple shield beside the message. The payload illustration can contain a status icon and a fault code icon. A shield illustrates the data policy; it must not imply a security certification.

Transition: The dashboard fault card becomes a service-status card. A small “Later” marker separates detection from an employee's physical service visit.

SCENE 7 — MAINTENANCE UPDATES — 16.5–18.5

Caption: “Stay informed during maintenance”

16.5–17.3: The same Air2Growth employee is back at the machine with a tool case. Show a safe, simple inspection gesture rather than internal wiring or a repair tutorial. The customer is nearby; the support engineer remains in a small linked panel.

17.3–18.5: The integrated screen updates from “Service needed” to “Maintenance in progress”. A notification/bell icon and the customer's acknowledgement show that they receive maintenance updates. Keep the privacy panel visible.

Do not invent SMS, email, a mobile application, remote repair capability, guaranteed response times, or an instant physical repair. Use the integrated touchscreen as the illustrated customer notification channel. Any other confirmed channels can be added later without changing the story.

Transition: Pull back from the service-status screen to the greenhouse and fields. The support card and technician remain part of the wider customer-support story; do not flash a “fixed” state without showing or establishing completion.

SCENE 8 — CLOSING FRAME — 18.5–20.0

The camera settles on a broad farm view: customer, Air2Growth employee, machine, greenhouse, growing plants, and the two background workers. Bring back the full supplied logo with its existing tagline “FROM CARBON TO CROP”. Keep the privacy panel legible and the machine's touchscreen visible.

At 20.0 seconds the animation stops and the final frame remains available to read. Show “Replay” and “Continue website”. Keep this hold outside the 20 seconds of motion. Reuse the closing illustration as the following normal-flow section so releasing the pin does not make the final image disappear.

HOW TO STITCH THE SCENES TOGETHER

Use one illustration world and one master timeline rather than eight independent animated pages. The machine, customer, employee, greenhouse, and logo must keep their identity across shots.

Use the following continuity anchors:
1. Order confirmation → label on the delivery crate.
2. Labelled crate → branded van → machine at the farm.
3. Installed machine exterior → explanatory cutaway.
4. Machine status light → attached touchscreen status.
5. Touchscreen operating state → IoT packet → support dashboard.
6. Dashboard fault → local service notification → maintenance status.
7. Service view → wider farm view with the original logo.

Allow approximately 0.2–0.35 seconds for a pan, wipe, or crossfade inside each allocated interval. Keep outgoing and incoming positions aligned around the shared object. Fade each caption in promptly and leave it still while the illustration moves. Put the most important action in the middle of each interval, not during the transition.

No independently running infinite animations should drive story actions. Wheels, hands, flow particles, status lights, and data packets must derive from the same timeline so manual reverse scrolling produces coherent frames. A deterministic time-based particle pattern is sufficient. Do not use random values that change on every render.

SCROLL AND PLAYBACK CONTRACT

Preferred implementation: GSAP with ScrollTrigger, using the project's supported, pinned dependency versions. Use a master timeline whose logical duration is exactly 20 seconds. ScrollTrigger can pin the stage and link that timeline to the section's scroll position with scrub: true. Animate children of the pinned stage, not the pin target itself. Use responsive setups, such as gsap.matchMedia(), for desktop, mobile, and reduced-motion behaviour.

The automatic scroll driver is a separate opt-in control layer. It advances the actual document scroll position linearly from the story start to its end over 20 seconds. ScrollTrigger reads that position and renders the matching frame. Do not also call timeline.play() while ScrollTrigger is controlling its progress; the driver and the timeline must have one source of truth.

Core mapping:
logicalTime = 20 × clamp((scrollY − storyStart) / (storyEnd − storyStart), 0, 1)

Use one scoped scroll runway, initially about four viewport heights on desktop, tuned after visual testing. Its distance determines pixels per second, not the story duration. Build the pin and runway on activation, measure the actual start/end after layout settles, align to the first story frame, and then start the 20-second clock. Reserve real space for the final frame and following website content.

Control behaviour:
- Begin: activate the tour, align its stage, focus the tour controls, and start guided scrolling. Never start or replay without a deliberate activation.
- Pause: stop the automatic scroll driver at the current position. The frame remains tied to the actual scroll position, so the visitor can then scrub manually.
- Wheel, touch scrolling, scrollbar dragging, or navigation keys: stop the driver immediately and allow native input. Do not interpret the driver's own programmatic scroll events as user intervention. Do not fight or cancel the visitor's scroll input.
- Resume: start from the current scroll progress. Remaining playback time is 20 × (1 − currentProgress) seconds.
- Escape or “Exit tour”: stop playback, remove the active pin/runway, preserve a sensible visual position, and restore normal page interaction. Return keyboard focus to the launch button. Exit must not force completion of the remaining story.
- Skip / “Continue website”: stop playback, remove the active pin/runway, move to the following website content, and give its heading appropriate focus.
- Completion: stop at 20 seconds, release guided control, and present the closing frame and controls in normal flow. The final frame must remain readable without a forced jump or loop.
- Replay: deliberately rebuild/reset the tour and begin at zero. Prevent duplicated listeners, pin spacers, or competing drivers.
- Hidden browser tab: pause elapsed playback, so returning does not skip the remaining scenes. Resume only after a clear user action.
- Resize/orientation change: preserve logical progress, recalculate positions, and leave the driver paused until the user resumes.

Keep Pause/Resume, Exit, and a progress indicator visible and keyboard accessible throughout the tour. Provide a Skip link. Do not hide or disable the page scrollbar, globally lock body scrolling, or trap keyboard focus. Scope event handlers and cleanup to this section. Respect existing site navigation and scroll behaviour elsewhere.

Controls and explanatory text should be HTML elements. Illustration layers can be SVG with named groups for people, machine, pipes, flows, van, data packets, dashboard, touchscreen, and logo. Keep all IDs scoped so the component can coexist with the website's other SVGs.

RESPONSIVE AND ACCESSIBLE PRESENTATION

Desktop: use a wide illustration stage with a stable caption area and controls. Mobile: recompose the scene into a portrait arrangement instead of shrinking the desktop artwork until the words become unreadable. Preserve the seven story actions, the integrated touchscreen, and the privacy message; reduce background scenery first.

Keep captions as real text. Do not put scene paragraphs into an SVG path. Make the privacy statement readable on mobile, with adequate contrast and space above the controls. Use a minimum comfortable body-text size, wrapping where needed. Keep the supplied logo at its original aspect ratio.

For prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, show a still sequence with Next/Previous controls and the same content. Do not auto-scroll, pan, or play the moving cutaway. Provide the text explanation below the illustration. If JavaScript or the animation library is unavailable, the website must still show the poster and explanation with normal scrolling.

Use accessible names for controls. Hide decorative illustration layers from assistive technology and provide the complete explanation once in real text. A scene label can be announced on meaningful scene changes, rather than announcing each animation frame or status packet.

STATIC EXPLANATION FOR THE WEBSITE

“You order an Air2Growth machine, and our employee delivers it, installs it on site, and shows you how to use it. The machine captures CO₂ from ambient air and supplies it to algae cultivation. With water, nutrients, and light, the algae grow. Harvesting and preparation produce liquid algae-based fertiliser. You can check operating status, production, tank levels, and energy on the integrated touchscreen. Through IoT, Air2Growth receives machine states and fault information so our team can monitor problems. You receive service notifications and updates during maintenance. Machine monitoring collects operating states and fault codes only, with no other private information from your site.”

Scope the privacy statement to machine monitoring. Ordering and service communication can require contact details; those are separate from the illustrated machine-state payload. Do not turn the caption into a blanket claim that the website never processes any personal information. Build the monitoring display around the stated machine-state-only policy.
